# Author style preferences

These are project preferences, not grammatical rules. Scientific meaning, technical precision, and valid English take precedence. Apply them to the requested passage without broad stylistic normalization.

- Use formal, concise scientific English with precise terminology and direct, declarative sentences.
- Preserve established technical terms rather than paraphrasing for variation.
- Prefer explicit agents and referents where they clarify responsibility or meaning.
- Introduce a tool with "This work uses X" and attribute a proposed mechanism to this work when appropriate.
- Avoid promotional wording, vague intensifiers, decorative phrasing, redundant scaffolding, and adjacent repetition.
- Preserve paragraph function and the intended logical sequence.
- Do not use em dashes or en dashes as prose punctuation.
- Do not use semicolons in manuscript prose unless explicitly requested.

Do not present these preferences as universal English rules. A grammatical construction warrants a finding only when it conflicts with a relevant preference or creates an identifiable comprehension problem.
